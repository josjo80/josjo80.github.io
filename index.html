    Move the arm.

    There is a LeRobot SO-101 on my desk: a small, cheap, open-source arm that thousands of people own, which means anything I learn on it someone else can reproduce. This is a simplified model of it. Drag the sliders, drag the view to look around, or let it run a pick and place.

    What I'm using it for

    Most of today's robot policies generate actions with a flow-matching head on top of a vision-language model. A recent method steers that head at inference time to make motion smoother, but it costs a second forward pass and uses one guidance strength everywhere, which can hurt precise moves.

    My project tries to fix both: get the guidance signal almost for free, and turn it up only when the policy is uncertain. The plan is to prove it in simulation on the LIBERO benchmark with the open π0 model, then confirm it on this arm.

    Notes from an Independent Research Bench

    Joshua Johnson
    Superior, Colorado

    Abstract. I do machine learning research on my own time, with rented GPUs and no lab. That constraint picks the problems: I look for ideas where the contribution is in the method rather than in the size of the dataset or the cluster. Three threads are active.

     

    1 A-CALM

    Language models predict one discrete token at a time. A-CALM, an anchored, adaptive-bandwidth continuous autoregressive language model, asks what happens if the model instead predicts a continuous vector that stands for a chunk of several tokens, and lets the amount of text per step vary. The work ran for months and produced a full PyTorch codebase, six rounds of written recommendations, and a draft journal paper.

    2 Chunk-amortized self-speculative decoding

    The current direction grew out of A-CALM. Speculative decoding speeds up generation by drafting cheaply and verifying with the full model. Here the model drafts for itself, a chunk at a time, so the cost of verification is spread over several tokens.

    cost per token ≈ (draft + verify) / accepted chunk length

    3 Cheap adaptive guidance for robot policies

    Inference-time guidance for the flow-based action heads used in vision-language-action models, tested with π0 on LIBERO. The robotics world on this map has a model of the arm it will end up on.

    Reproduce the baseline before writing anything new. Decide in advance what result would count. Write down what failed. Most of this is done in conversation with AI coding tools, which is its own experiment.

    The Sun God

    A book I'm writing for general readers about a question that bothered me for years: if the universe always slides toward disorder, why is it full of stars, cells, brains and cities?

    The short answer is that energy flowing from a hot place to a cold place can build things on the way through. The sun pours low-entropy light on the Earth, the Earth sheds heat to the night sky, and in between, order gets its chance. The book follows that thread from the first stars to the origin of life, the leap to complex cells, intelligence, civilization and AI.

    Try the coffee cup

    Pour cream on coffee. Disorder only ever goes up. But the interesting patterns show up in the middle, then vanish.

    Upper line: how mixed the cup is. Lower line: a rough measure of visible structure, the number of places where neighbouring patches differ. This is a toy with random stirring, so the hump is modest.

    Deal analysis

    When a company I'm a partner in explored a sale, I read the offering memorandum, studied the buyers, and modelled the offers side by side: a private equity buyout, an employee ownership plan, and a strategic buyer, each at different stake sizes. The answer that surprised people was that selling less could be worth more.

    Sell everything today: 1.00× today's value
    A deliberately simple picture: total value = share sold + share kept × growth. Real models add taxes, earn-outs, debt, control terms and the time value of money.

    Portfolio management

    I hold limited partner positions in venture and growth funds alongside public equities, alternatives and real estate. I look through every layer to see what I actually own, because the same bet can hide in four places at once. I run Monte Carlo projections and stress tests, track fund performance with MOIC, TVPI and public market equivalents, and model withdrawal strategies over thirty-year horizons.
